# Business Memo

## To: Product, Marketing, and Customer Success Teams

## Subject: Key Churn Risk Findings from Customer Analysis

Based on exploratory analysis of customer, order, support-ticket, and churn datasets, several patterns emerged that may help reduce customer churn.

### Key Findings

### 1. Lower Purchase Frequency is Associated with Higher Churn

Customers who churned generally placed fewer orders than customers who remained active. This suggests that declining purchase activity may be an early warning sign of churn risk.

### 2. Lower Customer Spending is Associated with Higher Churn

Customers with lower total spending were more likely to churn. Lower-value customers may require targeted engagement before they become inactive.

### 3. Churn Occurs Across All Demographic Segments

Customers from all age groups and city tiers showed churn behavior. Demographic characteristics alone do not appear sufficient for identifying churn risk.

### 4. Customer Support Experience Should Be Monitored

Support ticket volume alone did not strongly differentiate churned customers from retained customers. However, support-related metrics such as sentiment score, resolution time, and ticket reopening should be investigated further.

## Recommended Actions

1. Monitor customers with declining order frequency.
2. Create retention campaigns for low-spending customers.
3. Track customer engagement and purchasing behavior continuously.
4. Improve support resolution quality and customer satisfaction.
5. Develop a predictive churn model using behavioral signals rather than demographics alone.

## Priority Recommendation

The highest-priority retention opportunity is identifying customers with decreasing purchase frequency and lower spending levels, as these signals showed the strongest relationship with churn in the analysis.
